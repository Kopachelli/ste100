FIELD 1
Rule 1.12, technical verbs. Category 2, Computer processes and applications.

FIELD 2
Add a new subcategory 2 d) to rule 1.12. Use the same format as the subcategories 2 a) thru 2 c).

2. Computer processes and applications
Terms that give instructions and information for:
d) Artificial intelligence and machine learning processes:
annotate, embed, fine-tune, infer, label, prompt, train

Examples in STE:
Annotate the images before you train the model.
Train the model with the applicable data.
Prompt the model with the instructions for the task.
Fine-tune the model for your subject field.
The system embeds the text and keeps the result in the database.

FIELD 3
Issue 9 added artificial intelligence terms to rule 1.5, category 19. Examples are "artificial intelligence", "deep learning", "embedding", "hallucination", "large language model", "machine learning", "prompt engineering", "token", and "tuning".

Rule 1.12, category 2, does not include the related technical verbs. Its three subcategories give verbs for input and output processes, for user interface processes, and for system operations. No subcategory gives verbs for artificial intelligence processes.

Thus a writer who documents an AI-enabled system has only two options. Each option disagrees with a rule.

The first option is to use an approved word with a meaning that the dictionary does not approve. TUNE (v) has the approved meaning "to adjust equipment to the best performance". DEPLOY (v) has an approved meaning for movement out of a position of storage and into operation. A model is not equipment, and a model is not in storage. Thus "tune the model" and "deploy the model" disagree with rule 1.3. The risk is high, because rule 1.5 already gives the related nouns "tuning" and "artificial intelligence".

The second option is a noun construction for each action, for example "do the tuning of the model". Rule 1.12 correctly tells the writer to use an approved verb when the dictionary has one. But the dictionary has no approved verb for these actions. And rule 3.7 tells the writer to use a verb for an action, not a noun.

The proposed subcategory removes this condition. It is an addition to a structure that the standard already contains, and rule 1.12 tells the reader that the categories give examples only. Category 2 c) gives the precedent. It includes the verbs "boot", "debug", and "reboot", which the dictionary does not approve.

The change also agrees with the STEMG white paper on artificial intelligence of June 2026. That white paper says that STE must stay the primary reference in AI-related technical communication. A writer can only do this if STE gives the necessary words.
