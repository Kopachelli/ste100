# Descriptive writing — worked examples

Descriptions: 25 words maximum per sentence (6.3), no imperative, one topic per
paragraph (6.5), six sentences maximum (6.6), information given gradually (6.1),
key words repeated to connect sentences (6.2).

Every example on this page was written for this project.

---

## 1. Information given gradually

Rule 6.1. The failure is not length. It is that the reader meets four new things
before learning what any of them is.

**Before**

> Pressure from the accumulator is fed through the shuttle valve to the brake
> units, which are controlled by the anti-skid system that receives wheel speed
> data from the transducers mounted on each axle and modulates the pressure
> accordingly.

Findings: 41 words (6.3); five topics in one sentence (6.1, 4.1); passive voice
three times with the agent present (3.6); "-ing" forms (3.5).

**After**

> The accumulator supplies pressure to the brake units. The pressure goes through
> the shuttle valve. The anti-skid system controls the pressure to the brake
> units. A transducer on each axle sends the wheel speed to the anti-skid system.
> The anti-skid system uses this data to change the pressure.

Five sentences, each with one subject, each between 8 and 15 words. Every
sentence names something the previous sentence introduced: pressure, brake
units, anti-skid system, wheel speed. That chain is rule 6.2, and it is what
makes the second version readable rather than merely shorter.

## 2. Do not rotate synonyms

Rule 6.2, and rule 1.11 for the item names.

**Before**

> The controller monitors the flow. This module compares the reading against the
> setpoint. If the unit finds a difference, the device opens the bypass.

Four names — controller, module, unit, device — for one thing, or possibly four
things. The reader cannot tell which.

**After**

> The controller monitors the flow. The controller compares the flow against the
> setpoint. If the flow is different from the setpoint, the controller opens the
> bypass.

Repetition is not a defect in a description. It is the mechanism.

## 3. Passive voice when the agent is unknown

Rule 3.6 permits the passive in descriptive text, but only when nobody knows who
did it. This is a narrow permission and it is usually misapplied.

Correct:

> During transmission, the data was corrupted.

Nobody knows what corrupted the data. Forcing an active subject here would
invent one, and "Transmission corrupted the data" is technically false —
transmission is not the cause.

Not correct:

> The circuits are connected by a switching relay.

The agent is right there after "by". Write:

> A switching relay connects the circuits.

The test is the question "by whom or by what?". If the text answers it, the
sentence is passive and the answer is your subject.

## 4. Topic sentences

Rules 6.4 and 6.5. Reading only the first sentence of each paragraph should give
a usable outline.

**After**

> **The fuel manifold has three primary parts.** It has two shut-off valves, a
> pressure transducer, and three fittings.
>
> **The shut-off valve operates in the valve body.** The valve body holds a ball,
> a retaining ring, two seals, and a spring. The spring holds the ball in
> position to prevent internal leakage.
>
> **The pressure transducer is installed downstream of the shut-off valve.** A
> fitting connects the transducer to the manifold. The transducer has an
> electrical connector that connects it to the fuel management system.

Three topic sentences: what the parts are, how the valve works, where the
transducer sits. Each paragraph stays on its topic and none exceeds six
sentences.

## 5. Abstract into concrete

Rule 4.1. A sentence can obey every structural rule and still tell the reader
nothing.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Different temperatures will change the cure time. | When the temperature increases, the cure time decreases. |
| No leaks are permitted. | Make sure that there are no leaks. |
| Performance is affected by contamination. | Contamination in the fluid decreases the pump output. |

The first column is not wrong. It is empty. STE calls this out as a rule because
short abstract sentences pass a length check and fail the reader.

## 6. The multi-word noun

Rule 2.1. Three words maximum. Prepositions are how you get there.

| Before | Words | After |
|---|---|---|
| runway light connection resistance calibration | 5 | calibration of the resistance of the runway light connection |
| forward turbine overheat thermocouple terminal tags | 6 | the terminal tags on the forward overheat thermocouple of the turbine |
| engine transmission housing attachment bolts | 5 | the bolts that attach the transmission housing to the engine |

The rewritten version is longer in words and shorter in effort. In a five-word
stack the reader must hold four modifiers before reaching the head noun, and
must guess which modifier attaches to which — English puts the head noun last,
and many other languages put it first.

When the long form is the official term and cannot be changed, rule 2.2 applies:
write it in full once, then use a shorter form or an approved abbreviation, or
join the words that act as one unit with hyphens.

> Move the main-gear-door retraction-winch handle.

Three words by rule 8.7, because each hyphenated group counts as one.
