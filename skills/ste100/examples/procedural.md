# Procedural writing — worked examples

Procedures: 20 words maximum per sentence (5.1), one instruction per sentence
(5.2), imperative form (5.3), condition first (5.4).

Every example on this page was written for this project.

---

## 1. One long step becomes a numbered list

Rules 5.1, 5.2, 4.3.

**Before**

> To replace the filter cartridge, first shut off the inlet valve and then, after
> the pressure has dropped below 5 psi, unscrew the housing and remove the old
> cartridge, discarding it in the waste container provided.

Findings: 40 words in one procedural sentence (5.1); four instructions in one
sentence (5.2); present perfect "has dropped" (3.2); phrasal verbs "shut off"
and "unscrew ... and" (9.3); "-ing" form "discarding" (3.5).

**After**

> 1. Close the inlet valve.
> 2. Wait until the pressure decreases to less than 5 psi.
> 3. Remove the housing.
> 4. Remove the cartridge.
> 5. Discard the cartridge in the waste container.

Five steps of five to nine words each. The reader can stop after any one of them
and know where they are. That is what "one instruction per sentence" buys.

## 2. Condition first

Rule 5.4. The condition has to reach the reader before the action, because a
reader who has already started the action cannot un-start it.

**Before**

> Set the selector to STANDBY when the amber lamp comes on.

**After**

> When the amber lamp comes on, set the selector to STANDBY.

Both sentences are short and both obey every other rule. The second is correct
because the reader reads left to right.

Watch where the comma lands. These two are both grammatical and they mean
different things:

> If the pump does not operate correctly, disconnect it from the manifold.
> If the pump does not operate, correctly disconnect it from the manifold.

## 3. Passive voice into imperative

Rule 3.6, method 3. In a procedure the agent is always the reader, so the
imperative is available every time.

| Before | After | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| The test can be continued. | Continue the test. | 3.4, 3.6 |
| Oil and grease are to be removed with a degreasing agent. | Remove oil and grease with a degreasing agent. | 3.4, 3.6 |
| The temperature must be adjusted. | Adjust the temperature. | 3.4, 3.6 |
| The cover is to be installed before the seal. | Install the cover. Then install the seal. | 3.4, 5.2 |

## 4. Two actions in one sentence — when it is allowed

Rule 5.2 permits one sentence for two actions that occur at the same time, or
for a result that follows an action immediately.

Allowed:

> Hold the panel in the open position and install the fastener.
> Remove and discard the seal.

Not allowed, because the actions are sequential:

> Set the TEST switch to the middle position and release the SHORT-CIRCUIT TEST
> switch.

Split it:

> 1. Set the TEST switch to the middle position.
> 2. Release the SHORT-CIRCUIT TEST switch.

---

## 5. Do not omit words to get under the limit

Rule 4.2. This is the failure mode of every writer who has just been told about
a 20-word limit.

| Before | After | What was ambiguous |
|---|---|---|
| Rotary switch to INPUT. | Set the rotary switch to INPUT. | No verb, so no action |
| If installed, remove the shims. | If shims are installed, remove them. | "If **what** is installed?" |
| Remove the bolt and stop. | Remove the bolt and the stop. | "Stop" reads as a second command |
| Can be a maximum of five inches long. | Cracks can have a maximum length of five inches. | No subject |

The first version of each is shorter. None of them is clearer.

## 6. Consistent wording

Rule 9.4. Both of these obey every rule:

> Lubricate the two bolts with a small quantity of oil.
> Apply a small quantity of oil to the threads of the two bolts.

Using both in one procedure, for the same action, is still wrong. The reader
stops to work out whether two different things are being asked. Pick one and
repeat it.

The same applies to the item names. If the part is the "body assembly", it is
the body assembly in every step — not "main body" in step 1 and "body" in step 3
(rule 1.11).

## 7. A step that needs more than 20 words

Use the list. Rule 8.4 makes the colon end the sentence, so each item gets its
own 20-word allowance.

**Before**

> Before you install the pump, make sure that the O-rings, the gaskets, the
> filter screen, the drain plug and the mounting bolts are new and undamaged.

That is 27 words in a procedural sentence.

**After**

> Before you install the pump, make sure that these parts are new and not
> damaged:
> - The O-rings
> - The gaskets
> - The filter screen
> - The drain plug
> - The mounting bolts.

The lead-in is 14 words. Each item is two or three. Nothing was dropped.
