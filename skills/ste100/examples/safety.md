# Safety instructions — worked examples

Rules 7.1 thru 7.3, plus the 20-word limit of rule 5.1.

Every example on this page was written for this project. The uppercase is the
convention of the aerospace technical publication specifications, not an STE
rule — STE does not regulate formatting.

---

## The three decisions

1. **Which word?** (7.1) Warning for a risk of injury or death. Caution for a
   risk of damage to objects. Both risks present means warning.
2. **What must the reader do?** (7.2) A command or a condition, first.
3. **What happens if they do not?** (7.3) Name the consequence.

A safety instruction that gets 1 wrong is dangerous. One that gets 2 or 3 wrong
is merely ignored, which in practice is the same thing.

---

## 1. Wrong level of risk

Rule 7.1. This is the error that matters.

**Before**

> CAUTION: KEEP THE NITROGEN LINE FITTINGS FREE OF OIL.

Marked as a caution, so the reader treats it as a risk to equipment. Oil in a
high-pressure oxidizer line does not damage equipment. It ignites.

**After**

> WARNING: MAKE SURE THAT THE NITROGEN LINE FITTINGS ARE FULLY CLEAN AND HAVE NO
> OIL. OIL AND HIGH-PRESSURE GAS CAN CAUSE AN EXPLOSION. AN EXPLOSION CAN CAUSE
> INJURY OR DEATH.

Three sentences of 17, 11 and 7 words. Each is inside the 20-word limit. The
label is not counted.

No tool can make this correction. Deciding the level of risk needs a person who
knows the system.

---

## 2. Abstract statement instead of a command

Rule 7.2.

**Before**

> CAUTION: CARE SHOULD BE TAKEN WHEN HANDLING THE DISPLAY BEZEL.

Findings: no command (7.2); no consequence (7.3); passive voice (3.6); "should"
(the dictionary directs you to "must"); "-ing" form (3.5).

"Care should be taken" does not tell anyone what to do.

**After**

> CAUTION: HOLD THE DISPLAY BEZEL BY ITS EDGES. PRESSURE ON THE FACE OF THE
> BEZEL CAN CAUSE DAMAGE TO THE DISPLAY.

Now there is one thing to do and one reason to do it.

---

## 3. No consequence given

Rule 7.3.

**Before**

> WARNING: DO NOT SWALLOW THE SOLVENT.

The command is clear. The reader has no idea how serious it is, and a reader who
does not know the stakes takes a shortcut eventually.

**After**

> WARNING: DO NOT SWALLOW THE SOLVENT. ALWAYS MAKE SURE THAT YOU KNOW THE SAFETY
> PRECAUTIONS AND THE FIRST AID INSTRUCTIONS FOR SOLVENTS. SOLVENTS ARE POISONOUS
> AND CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH.

---

## 4. Condition before command

Rule 7.2 again, and rule 5.4.

**Before**

> WARNING: POINT THE SPRAY AWAY FROM YOUR FACE WHILE YOU USE THE SPRAY PAINT.
> INJURY TO YOUR EYES CAN OCCUR.

**After**

> WARNING: WHILE YOU USE THE SPRAY PAINT, POINT THE SPRAY AWAY FROM YOUR FACE.
> THE SPRAY PAINT CAN CAUSE INJURY TO YOUR EYES.

The condition sets the scene before the command arrives.

---

## 5. Negative commands in a list

Rule 4.3. Repeat the negative on each item, so that every item is correct when
it is read alone. Readers scan lists; they do not always carry the lead-in down
the page.

**Before**

> CAUTION: WHEN YOU GET ACCESS THROUGH THE REAR PANEL, DO NOT:
> - PUT YOUR FEET ON THE HYDRAULIC LINE.
> - USE THE HYDRAULIC LINE AS A HANDLE.

**After**

> CAUTION: WHEN YOU GET ACCESS THROUGH THE REAR PANEL:
> - DO NOT PUT YOUR FEET ON THE HYDRAULIC LINE.
> - DO NOT USE THE HYDRAULIC LINE AS A HANDLE.
>
> THE HYDRAULIC LINE CAN BREAK AND CAUSE INJURY.

---

## 6. A note that is really a warning

Rule 5.5. If the information prevents injury or damage, it is not a note. Notes
get skipped, and this one must not be.

**Before**

> NOTE: Before closing the hatch, it is mandatory to ensure that no persons are
> in the crew rest compartment. When the hatch is closed there is no airflow to
> the compartment and therefore there is a risk of suffocation.

**After**

> WARNING: BEFORE YOU CLOSE THE HATCH, MAKE SURE THAT NO PERSONS ARE IN THE CREW
> REST COMPARTMENT. WHEN THE HATCH IS CLOSED, THERE IS NO AIRFLOW TO THE
> COMPARTMENT. THERE IS A RISK OF SUFFOCATION.

---

## What the checker can and cannot do here

It checks that a safety block starts with a command or a condition (7.2), that
it names a consequence (7.3), and that no sentence is longer than 20 words
(5.1).

It cannot check rule 7.1. Choosing between a warning and a caution is a risk
analysis, and the text gives no evidence either way. When you audit safety
instructions, read every one and ask what actually happens.
